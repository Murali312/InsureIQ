# General Health Insurance FAQs — India

This document provides comprehensive, plain-English educational guidance on how health insurance operates in India under the regulatory supervision of the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI).

---

## 1. Health Insurance Basics and How Policies Work

### What is health insurance?
Health insurance is a legal contract between an individual (the policyholder/insured) and an insurance company (the insurer). In exchange for regular payment known as an **annual premium**, the insurer agrees to pay or reimburse admissible medical expenses incurred due to illness, disease, injury, or unexpected hospitalisation, subject to the policy terms, conditions, sub-limits, waiting periods, and exclusions.

### Core Principles of Health Insurance:
1. **Principle of Indemnity:** Health insurance (indemnity plans) compensates you for the actual financial loss incurred up to the Sum Insured. It is designed to restore you to your financial position prior to hospitalization, not to generate profit. (Note: Benefit policies like Critical Illness plans pay a fixed lump-sum amount upon diagnosis regardless of actual hospital expense).
2. **Utmost Good Faith (*Uberrimae Fidei*):** The applicant must honestly disclose all pre-existing medical conditions, past surgeries, habits, and family medical history on the proposal form.
3. **Insurable Interest:** You can insure yourself, your spouse, dependent children, dependent parents, and legal dependents for whom you bear financial responsibility.

## 2. Individual Health Insurance vs. Family Floater Policies

| Feature | Individual Health Insurance | Family Floater Health Insurance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Coverage Scope** | Dedicated, separate Sum Insured for each covered individual. | A single, shared Sum Insured accessible by all covered family members. |
| **Sum Insured Usage** | Member A using ₹5 Lakhs does not affect Member B's ₹5 Lakhs limit. | If Member A utilizes ₹7 Lakhs from a ₹10 Lakhs floater, ₹3 Lakhs remains for the entire family for the rest of that policy year (unless restoration applies). |
| **Premium Determination** | Calculated individually based on each member's specific age and health risk. | Calculated primarily based on the age and risk profile of the **eldest family member**. |
| **Best Suited For** | - Senior citizens (60+ years)<br>- Members with known chronic conditions<br>- Adults requiring dedicated high-value coverage | - Young nuclear families (parents + 1 or 2 children)<br>- Healthy couples with no major pre-existing illnesses |
| **Recommendation Tip** | Keep parents on their own individual policies. Adding parents to a family floater drastically inflates the floater premium and increases the chance of exhausting the shared sum insured. |

## 3. Sum Insured, Cumulative Bonus, and Restoration Benefits

### Sum Insured (SI)
The Sum Insured is the maximum financial cap payable by the insurer for all admissible hospitalization claims during a single policy year.
- **Base SI:** The primary coverage chosen at inception (e.g., ₹5 Lakhs, ₹10 Lakhs, ₹25 Lakhs, ₹1 Crore).

### Cumulative Bonus (No Claim Bonus / NCB)
Modern Indian health insurance policies reward claim-free years by increasing the Sum Insured without increasing your base premium:
- Standard policies increase the sum insured by **10% to 50%** per claim-free year, up to 100% of base SI.
- Enhanced "Super NCB" plans offered by insurers like Care Health and HDFC ERGO can increase coverage by **up to 500%** over consecutive claim-free renewals.

### Restoration / Refill / ReAssure Benefit
If your base sum insured is exhausted or partially depleted during a policy year due to claims, the restoration feature automatically reinstates **100% of the base sum insured**:
- **Same Illness vs. Different Illness:** Traditional restoration required the subsequent hospitalization to be for an unrelated illness. Modern policies (e.g., Care Supreme, Niva Bupa ReAssure 2.0, HDFC ERGO Optima Secure) offer **unlimited restoration for any illness (related or unrelated)**, provided it occurs in separate hospitalization events.

## 4. Key Cost-Sharing Terms: Deductibles, Co-payments, Sub-limits, and Room Rent

### What is a Deductible?
A **deductible** is a pre-agreed threshold amount that the policyholder must pay out of pocket before the insurance company starts paying the claim.
- *Example:* On a policy with a ₹50,000 deductible and a ₹2,00,000 hospital bill, you pay the first ₹50,000, and the insurer pays the remaining ₹1,50,000 (subject to admissible terms).
- *Top-Up / Super Top-Up Plans:* Use high deductibles (e.g., ₹5 Lakhs) to provide cost-effective massive coverage (e.g., ₹20 Lakhs) at a fraction of the cost of base insurance.

### What is a Co-Payment (Co-Pay)?
A **co-payment** is a fixed percentage of each admissible claim amount that the policyholder must bear, regardless of how high the bill is.
- *Example:* On a ₹1,00,000 bill with a **20% co-payment**, the policyholder pays ₹20,000, and the insurer pays ₹80,000.
- Co-payments are common in senior citizen plans or zonal pricing structures. Wherever possible, buyers should choose **0% co-pay** or buy a co-pay waiver rider.

### What is Room Rent Capping and Proportionate Deduction?
**Room Rent Capping** restricts the room category you can occupy during hospitalization:
- Typically defined as a percentage (e.g., **1% of Sum Insured per day** for normal rooms, **2% for ICU**). On a ₹5 Lakh policy, 1% equals ₹5,000 per day.
- **The Danger of Proportionate Deduction:** If you choose a room costing ₹10,000/day when your policy limit is ₹5,000/day (a 50% breach), the insurer reduces **all associated medical expenses proportionately by 50%** (surgeon fees, nursing, doctor visits, operation theatre charges), resulting in huge unexpected out-of-pocket costs.
- **Golden Rule:** Always choose a policy with **No Room Rent Capping** or "Single Private AC Room" entitlement.

### What are Treatment Sub-Limits?
A sub-limit places a maximum cap on specific treatments regardless of your overall sum insured:
- Common examples: Cataract surgery (capped at ₹25,000–₹40,000 per eye), Knee replacement (capped at ₹1.5–₹2 Lakhs per joint), Kidney dialysis, Hernia surgeries, or robotic procedures.

## 5. Waiting Periods and Pre-Existing Diseases (PED)

### What is a Pre-Existing Disease (PED)?
Under IRDAI regulations, a PED is any condition, ailment, injury, or disease diagnosed by a physician or for which medical advice or treatment was recommended or received within **36 months (3 years)** prior to the effective date of the policy.

### Waiting Periods in Indian Health Insurance:
1. **Initial 30-Day Waiting Period:** No claims are payable for any illness during the first 30 days of a new policy, **except for accidental injuries** requiring immediate emergency hospitalization.
2. **Specific Ailment Waiting Period (1 to 2 Years):** A statutory waiting period for slow-developing conditions, regardless of whether diagnosed previously (e.g., cataract, hernia, piles, hydrocele, joint replacement, hysterectomy, sinus surgery, benign prostatic hypertrophy).
3. **Pre-Existing Disease (PED) Waiting Period:** Under the **IRDAI 2024 Master Circular**, the maximum waiting period insurers can mandate for pre-existing diseases is **capped at 36 months (3 years)**, down from the previous 48 months. Many modern insurers now offer 1-year or 2-year PED reduction riders.
4. **Maternity Waiting Period:** Typically ranges from **24 to 48 months**; maternity expenses are generally excluded from standard individual base covers unless a dedicated rider is attached.

## 6. Exclusions and Non-Medical Expenses

### Standard Exclusions in Health Insurance:
- Aesthetic, cosmetic, or plastic surgery (unless reconstructive surgery following an accident or burn).
- Intentional self-inflicted injury, suicide attempts, or injury sustained while under the influence of alcohol or narcotics.
- Experimental, unproven, or investigational medical treatments.
- Expenses related to war, invasion, radiation, or nuclear contamination.
- Hazardous or adventure sports (skydiving, paragliding, scuba diving) unless covered by a dedicated sports rider.
- Domiciliary hospitalization that does not meet statutory criteria (e.g., unprescribed home rest).

### Non-Medical / Non-Payable Expenses:
Hospitals bill for consumable items that are traditionally non-admissible under IRDAI guidelines (e.g., surgical gloves, syringes, PPE kits, admission charges, administrative fees, food/beverage for attendants, toiletry kits).
- *Consumer Tip:* Many insurers now offer a **"Claim Protect" or "Consumables Cover" rider** that covers all 68+ IRDAI non-medical items, reducing out-of-pocket expenses to virtually zero.

## 7. Network Hospitals, Cashless Claims, and Reimbursement Process

### Cashless Hospitalization:
- **How it works:** The insured is treated at an empanelled **Network Hospital**. The hospital's Third-Party Administrator (TPA) / Insurance Help Desk directly liaises with the insurer.
- **Pre-Authorization:**
  - *Planned Hospitalization:* Submit pre-authorization form 48 to 72 hours before admission.
  - *Emergency Hospitalization:* Hospital TPA desk submits pre-authorization within 24 hours of admission.
- **Mandated IRDAI Timelines (2024 Master Circular):**
  - **1 Hour:** Insurers/TPAs must communicate initial cashless authorization within 1 hour of receiving the request from the hospital.
  - **3 Hours:** Insurers/TPAs must communicate final claim assessment and discharge approval within 3 hours of receiving the final bill and discharge summary from the hospital.
  - In case of delays, the hospital cannot retain the patient, and any additional room charges caused by insurer delay must be borne by the insurer.

### Reimbursement Claims:
- **When is reimbursement used?** When treatment occurs at a **Non-Network Hospital** or when cashless pre-authorization was denied or not opted for.
- **Step-by-Step Procedure:**
  1. Pay the hospital bills in full upon discharge.
  2. Collect all original documents: Duly filled claim form, discharge summary, itemized hospital bill, payment receipts, doctor prescriptions, diagnostic reports, and medicine purchase receipts.
  3. Submit the claim packet to the insurer or TPA within **15 to 30 days** of discharge.
  4. The insurer investigates and processes the claim within 15–30 days of receiving all valid documents.

## 8. Policy Lifecycle: Free Look, Renewal, Portability, and Migration

### Free Look Period:
- Policyholders have **30 days** for policies purchased electronically/online or through distance marketing (15 days for physical policies) from receipt of the policy document to review the clauses.
- If you find the terms unsatisfactory, you can cancel the policy and receive a full refund of premium, minus nominal deductions for stamp duty, proportional risk cover for the period, and medical checkup expenses incurred by the insurer.

### Grace Period and Renewal:
- Health insurance contracts are valid for 1, 2, or 3 years.
- Insurers grant a **grace period of 30 days** (15 days for monthly payment modes) for renewal after the expiry date.
- During the grace period, coverage is paused (no claims are payable for events happening in the grace period), but your accrued waiting period credits and Cumulative Bonus are **protected** if paid before the grace period ends.

### Portability (Switching Insurers):
- Under IRDAI Portability regulations, you have the statutory right to switch from one insurance company to another without losing accrued benefits (such as completed waiting periods and Cumulative Bonus).
- **Application Window:** You must initiate the portability request with the new insurer at least **45 days prior to your policy renewal date**.

### Migration (Switching Plans Within Same Insurer):
- The right to change plans, sum insured, or variants within your existing insurance company while retaining all waiting period credits.

## 9. Claim Rejections, Grievance Redressal, and Escalation Route

### Common Reasons for Claim Rejection:
1. **Non-disclosure of pre-existing diseases** on the initial proposal form.
2. Treatment sought for ailments during an active waiting period (e.g., 30-day initial period or specific illness waiting period).
3. Admission purely for diagnostic tests without active medical management.
4. Permanent exclusions (e.g., aesthetic treatments, unapproved therapies).
5. Inordinate delay in submitting reimbursement claim documents without valid justification.

### Step-by-Step Grievance Escalation Process:
- **Level 1 — Insurer Grievance Redressal Officer (GRO):** Contact your insurer's official GRO (contact details must be printed on your policy schedule and Customer Information Sheet). The insurer has **14 days** to address and resolve the grievance.
- **Level 2 — IRDAI Bima Bharosa Grievance Portal:** If the insurer fails to respond within 14 days or provides an unsatisfactory resolution, register your complaint on the IRDAI **Bima Bharosa Portal** (https://bimabharosa.irdai.gov.in) or call Toll-Free **155255** / **1800 4254 732**.
- **Level 3 — Insurance Ombudsman:**
  - If the grievance remains unresolved for **30 days**, or if the insurer rejects the complaint, you can approach the **Insurance Ombudsman** within your territorial jurisdiction.
  - The Ombudsman handles disputes up to **₹50 Lakhs** for individual policyholders.
  - The process is free of cost, informal, and awards are legally binding on the insurance company.

## 10. Policy Documents: How to Read the Policy Schedule, CIS, and Wording

1. **Policy Schedule:** The summary certificate showing the policyholder name, insured members, policy tenure, Sum Insured, deductible/co-pay (if any), premium paid, and endorsements.
2. **Customer Information Sheet (CIS):** IRDAI mandates that insurers provide a standardized 1–2 page CIS with every policy. It clearly highlights in plain text:
   - What the policy covers.
   - What the policy excludes.
   - Exact waiting periods applicable.
   - Sub-limits and deductibles.
   - Free look and cancellation clauses.
   - Contact details for complaints and grievance officers.
3. **Policy Wording (Contract):** The complete legal document detailing every definition, inclusion, exclusion, procedure, and legal condition governing the policy.
