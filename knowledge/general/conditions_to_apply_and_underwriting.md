# Conditions to Apply for Health Insurance in India — Eligibility and Underwriting Guide

This document outlines the essential eligibility criteria, underwriting mechanisms, required documentation, and special considerations for individuals applying for health insurance in India under IRDAI regulations.

---

## 1. Age and General Eligibility Requirements

### Age Criteria:
- **Adults:** Minimum entry age is **18 years**.
- **Removal of Maximum Entry Age Limit (IRDAI 2024 Reform):** Following the **IRDAI Master Circular on Health Insurance Business (May 2024)**, insurers can **no longer impose an upper age limit** for purchasing health insurance. Senior citizens aged 65, 70, 75, 80, or older are legally entitled to apply for health insurance. Insurers are required to offer products suited to diverse age demographics.
- **Children:** Dependent children can typically be covered from **91 days to 25 years** (as dependent unmarried children or students). In policies offering maternity riders, newborn infants may be covered from **Day 1** of birth.
- **Insurable Interest:** You can purchase health coverage for yourself, your spouse, dependent children, dependent parents, parents-in-law, or legal wards.

## 2. Can a Person with Diabetes, High Blood Pressure (Hypertension), or Asthma Apply?

### YES, people with pre-existing chronic conditions CAN apply.
Having chronic conditions such as Type-2 diabetes, hypertension, asthma, thyroid disorders, or high cholesterol does not automatically disqualify you from getting health insurance in India. However, policy issuance and coverage terms depend on **medical underwriting**:

### Potential Underwriting Outcomes:
1. **Standard Acceptance:** If conditions are well-controlled (e.g., normal HbA1c, healthy blood pressure readings) and lifestyle risk is low, some insurers issue the policy at standard premium rates.
2. **Medical Loading (Premium Increase):** Insurers may accept the proposal with a **premium loading** (e.g., charging 10% to 30% higher premium) to account for higher anticipated hospitalization risk.
3. **Pre-Existing Disease (PED) Waiting Period:** Any claim arising directly from or related to the declared condition will be subject to a waiting period of **12 to 36 months** (capped at maximum 36 months / 3 years under IRDAI 2024 regulations). Once this waiting period is served, full hospitalization coverage applies.
4. **Permanent Exclusion / Co-payment Riders:** In some instances, an insurer may offer coverage for all other illnesses while excluding the specific pre-existing condition (where permitted by IRDAI guidelines) or by applying a mandatory co-payment on claims related to that ailment.
5. **Dedicated Chronic Illness Plans:** Insurers offer specialized plans designed specifically for individuals with chronic conditions:
   - *Aditya Birla Activ Health Platinum Enhanced:* Day-1 coverage for diabetes, hypertension, asthma, and hyperlipidemia under chronic management programs.
   - *Star Diabetes Safe Insurance:* Dedicated plan for Type-1 and Type-2 diabetes patients covering complications and hospitalizations.
   - *Care Freedom:* Plan designed for diabetic or hypertensive individuals with no mandatory pre-policy medical checkup.
6. **Rejection / Postponement:** If chronic conditions are uncontrolled or accompanied by multiple severe organ complications (e.g., advanced diabetic nephropathy, severe cardiovascular blockages), the insurer may postpone or decline the application.

*Important:* Acceptance and premium rates are determined solely by each insurer's independent underwriting board. Never assume that rejection by one insurer means another will not accept you.

## 3. The Mandatory Duty of Utmost Good Faith (Uberrimae Fidei) & Section 45

### Strict Medical History Disclosure:
Under Indian insurance law, the insurance contract is based on the principle of **Utmost Good Faith (*Uberrimae Fidei*)**. The proposer is legally required to declare all known material facts regarding:
- Past illnesses, chronic conditions, and long-term medications.
- Surgeries, hospital admissions, or diagnostic investigations undergone.
- Tobacco, smoking, and alcohol habits.
- Family history of hereditary diseases (e.g., heart disease, cancer, diabetes).

### Section 45 of the Insurance Act (The 5-Year Moratorium Period):
- Under IRDAI regulations and Section 45, after a policy has completed **60 continuous months (5 years)** of coverage (including renewals and transferred periods under portability), **no claim or policy can be repudiated or questioned** by the insurer on the grounds of misstatement or non-disclosure of medical history, except in cases of proven fraud.
- Conversely, during the initial 5-year period, any concealed material fact discovered by the insurer during a claim investigation will result in **claim repudiation, forfeiture of premiums, and immediate policy termination**.

## 4. Documents Required to Apply for Health Insurance in India

Applying for an individual or family health insurance policy typically requires the following documents:

| Document Category | Purpose | Accepted Proofs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Proposal Form** | Contractual declaration of personal, health, and family details. | Duly completed and digitally or physically signed application form. |
| **2. Proof of Identity (PoI)** | Verification of applicant & insured members' legal identity. | - PAN Card (Mandatory under CKYC norms for financial transactions)<br>- Aadhaar Card<br>- Passport<br>- Voter ID Card |
| **3. Proof of Address (PoA)** | Verification of residence (determines premium zone in zonal pricing). | - Aadhaar Card<br>- Passport<br>- Recent Utility Bill (Electricity, Water, Gas)<br>- Driving License |
| **4. Proof of Age** | Accurate determination of age band and premium calculation. | - Birth Certificate<br>- 10th Standard School Certificate / Marksheet<br>- PAN Card / Passport |
| **5. Medical Records (if applicable)** | Risk evaluation for applicants declaring pre-existing conditions. | - Recent prescription slips<br>- Diagnostic test reports (HbA1c, lipid profile, ECG, liver function)<br>- Past hospital discharge summaries |
| **6. Portability Documents (if switching)** | Transferring accrued waiting period credits from existing insurer. | - Copy of previous year's policy schedule<br>- Renewal notice and claim history declaration form |
| **7. Income Proof (for high Sum Insured)** | Financial underwriting for high-value covers (> ₹25 Lakhs to ₹1 Crore). | - Form 16<br>- Recent Salary Slips (last 3 months)<br>- Income Tax Returns (ITR) |

## 5. Underwriting Process and Pre-Policy Medical Checkup (PPMC)

### Tele-Underwriting / Medical Video Call:
For applicants with clean medical histories or younger age profiles, policies are frequently issued instantly via automated rules and brief tele-underwriting calls.

### Pre-Policy Medical Examination (PPMC):
- **When is it triggered?**
  - Applicants aged **45–50 years and above** (depending on the insurer's policy terms).
  - High Sum Insured applications (₹25 Lakhs to ₹1 Crore+).
  - Applicants who declare chronic ailments (diabetes, high BP, heart disease, thyroid disorders).
- **Standard PPMC Test Battery:**
  - Blood Sugar Tests (Fasting and Post-Prandial / HbA1c)
  - Lipid Profile (Cholesterol, Triglycerides)
  - Kidney Function Test (Serum Creatinine, BUN) & Liver Function Test (LFT)
  - Resting ECG (Electrocardiogram)
  - Complete Blood Count (CBC) and Urine Routine
- **Cost of Medical Checkups:** Under IRDAI guidelines, if the proposal is accepted and the policy is issued, the insurer typically covers 50% to 100% of the medical checkup costs, or reimburses it after policy issuance.

## 6. How to Apply for Health Insurance in India

Consumers can purchase health insurance through multiple official, regulated channels:
1. **Direct Online Portal of the Insurer:** Official websites (e.g., www.hdfcergo.com, www.starhealth.in, www.careinsurance.com). Digital purchase allows instant policy issuance, transparent premium calculation, and access to the 30-day Free Look period.
2. **Authorized Insurance Agents:** IRDAI-licensed POSP or individual agents who assist with documentation, proposal filling, and claims guidance.
3. **IRDAI-Registered Insurance Brokers:** Web aggregators and corporate brokerage houses (e.g., Policybazaar, Coverfox, Beshak) that offer side-by-side product comparisons.
4. **Bancassurance (Partner Banks):** Public and private banks partnering with general and health insurers to distribute policies to their account holders.

## 7. Why Eligibility, Premiums, and Conditions Vary by Insurer

Insurance is not one-size-fits-all:
- **Risk Appetites Differ:** Some insurers specialize in retail retail mass-market covers (e.g., Star Health), while others specialize in high-sum-insured comprehensive products with global coverage (e.g., Care Supreme, HDFC Optima Secure).
- **Zonal Pricing:** Premiums vary based on the city of residence. Metro cities (Tier-1: Mumbai, Delhi-NCR) have higher treatment costs and therefore higher base premiums than Tier-2 or Tier-3 towns.
- **Underwriting Philosophy:** One insurer may accept an applicant with controlled diabetes with a 15% loading, while another may offer a 2-year waiting period, and a third may decline. Comparing multiple insurers allows consumers to find the most favorable terms.
