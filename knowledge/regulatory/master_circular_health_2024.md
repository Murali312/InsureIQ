# IRDAI Master Circular on Health Insurance Business (2024)

- **Issuing Authority:** Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI)
- **Reference Number:** IRDAI/HLT/REG/CIR/06/05/2024
- **Date of Issue:** 29th May 2024
- **Jurisdiction:** India
- **Applicability:** All General and Health Insurers (Life Insurers where health products are offered)
- **Official Source URL:** https://irdai.gov.in/web/guest/circulars?filterDepartment=HLT&filtersApplied=true

---

## 1. Customer Information Sheet (CIS)
Insurers must provide a standardized Customer Information Sheet (CIS) along with the policy document in simple, clear language and legible font size.
The CIS must clearly state:
1. Name and UIN of the insurance product.
2. Coverage details, sum insured, and deductible/co-payment obligations.
3. Waiting periods: specific pre-existing disease (PED) waiting period and specific ailment waiting periods.
4. Exclusions (sub-limits, specific conditions not covered).
5. Financial limits: room rent capping, ICU capping, co-pays, and deductibles.
6. Claims procedure: step-by-step guidance for cashless and reimbursement claims.
7. Grievance redressal mechanism: contact details of the Insurer's Grievance Redressal Officer (GRO) and the Insurance Ombudsman.

---

## 2. Timelines for Cashless Claims Processing
To improve policyholder experience during hospitalization:
1. **Initial Cashless Authorization:** Insurers / TPAs must decide on initial cashless authorization within **1 hour** of receiving the cashless request from the network hospital.
2. **Final Discharge Authorization:** Insurers / TPAs must decide on the final discharge and authorization within **3 hours** of receiving the final bill and discharge summary from the hospital.
3. In case of hospital mortality, the process for immediate release of the mortal remains must be handled with utmost priority without delaying for settlement formalities.

---

## 3. Pre-Existing Diseases (PED) and Waiting Period Reductions
1. **Maximum PED Waiting Period:** The maximum waiting period for pre-existing disease (PED) is capped at **36 months (3 years)**. Insurers cannot mandate a 48-month PED waiting period.
2. **Moratorium Period:** The moratorium period is reduced from 8 years to **60 continuous months (5 years)**. After 60 months of continuous coverage, no health insurance policy can be contested on grounds of non-disclosure or misrepresentation, except for proven fraud.

---

## 4. Inclusivity and Age Limits
1. Insurers must design and offer health insurance products catering to all age brackets, including senior citizens, without imposing arbitrary maximum entry age ceilings.
2. Dedicated products or riders must be considered for persons with disabilities (PwD) and specific chronic health conditions.

---

## 5. Portability and Continuity Benefits
1. Policyholders can port their indemnity health insurance policy from one insurer to another without losing accrued waiting period credits or cumulative bonus benefits, provided there is no break in coverage.
2. Portability requests must be initiated at least 45 days prior to policy renewal date.

---

## 6. Treatment under AYUSH Systems of Medicine
Insurers shall place treatments taken under AYUSH (Ayurveda, Yoga and Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha, and Homeopathy) on par with conventional modern medicine treatments, subject to treatment being undergone in recognized government or accredited healthcare institutions.

---

## 7. Cancellation and Free Look Period
1. Policyholders are entitled to a **free look period of 30 days** (from date of receipt of physical or electronic policy document) to review policy terms and conditions.
2. If no claim has occurred during this period, the policyholder may cancel and receive premium refund after deduction of proportionate risk premium, medical examination costs, and stamp duty.
