# IRDAI Health Insurance Regulations FAQs

- **Publisher:** Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI)
- **Source URL:** https://irdai.gov.in/faqs-on-health-insurance-regulations
- **Document Type:** regulatory_faq
- **Jurisdiction:** India
- **Status:** Current official regulatory guidance

---

## FAQ 1: What is the role of a Third Party Administrator (TPA) in health insurance?
A Third Party Administrator (TPA) is licensed by IRDAI to provide health insurance claim facilitation and processing services on behalf of insurance companies. 
Policyholders may receive cashless services or file reimbursement claims through the appointed TPA, or directly through the insurer's in-house claim processing department. 
The insurer remains legally accountable for all claim decisions and compliance with regulatory timelines.

---

## FAQ 2: What is the grace period for renewal of health insurance?
A grace period of **30 days** is provided for yearly renewal of health insurance policies. 
Payment made during this period ensures continuity of coverage benefits, including waiting period credits for pre-existing diseases. 
However, any hospitalization or illness occurring during the period between expiry date and actual renewal payment is not covered.

---

## FAQ 3: What is the difference between Cashless Facility and Reimbursement?
- **Cashless Facility:** The insurer settles admissible hospital expenses directly with the network healthcare provider upon authorization, subject to policy deductions, co-pays, and sub-limits.
- **Reimbursement:** The insured pays the hospital bills directly upon discharge and submits original documents, bills, prescriptions, and discharge summary to the insurer/TPA for post-hospitalization reimbursement review.

---

## FAQ 4: Can an insurer reject a health claim without written reasons?
No. IRDAI regulations strictly mandate that every claim repudiation or deduction must be communicated in writing with specific policy clauses, exclusions, or medical grounds cited. 
Arbitrary or verbal denials are prohibited under regulatory standards.

---

## FAQ 5: What is the Free Look Period?
The Free Look Period is a regulatory right granted to policyholders to examine the terms and conditions of a new policy. 
If the policyholder disagrees with any terms, they may cancel within 30 days of receiving the policy document, provided no claim has been registered. 
The insurer refunds premium after deducting reasonable medical test costs, proportionate risk charges, and statutory stamp duty.
